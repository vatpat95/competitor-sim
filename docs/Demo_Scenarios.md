# CompetitorSim — Demo Scenarios Reference

Three telecom scenarios featuring the **same competitive market and the same three
competitors** — but different TelcoX strategic decisions. This is the core proof of
concept: the same competitive field reacts completely differently depending on what
move TelcoX makes.

Click **Load Demo** in the app to get a randomly selected scenario, or copy the
data below to manually load any specific one.

## Scenario 1 — Direct Price Cut
**TelcoX strategic move:** 10% price cut on the core unlimited plan
**Expected verdict: REVISE STRATEGY**

**Why:** ValueNet (18.5% growth, strong cash, three consecutive aggressive price cuts)
will immediately out-discount TelcoX by going to 12–15% below. TelcoX bleeds margin
without winning durable share. PremiumConnect sits it out — a price cut doesn't
threaten their enterprise customers. RegionalPlus, already on 4.2x leverage, quietly
retreats further into rural niches. The net outcome is a margin-eroding price war
that TelcoX cannot win, and the orchestrator recommends a value-add alternative.

## Scenario 2 — Premium Bundle Launch
**TelcoX strategic move:** Launch 'TelcoX ONE' — unlimited + streaming + cloud + device insurance at 15% above base
**Expected verdict: PROCEED WITH CAUTION**

**Why:** This is not a price war — it's a value-stack play. ValueNet (pure price
competitor, no content licensing experience) cannot easily replicate bundled streaming
partnerships within the 18-month exclusivity window. RegionalPlus (4.2x debt, shrinking
headcount) cannot afford content licensing costs. But PremiumConnect — with 31.5%
EBITDA margins, 0.9x debt, and 11% R&D spend — absolutely can and will launch a
counter-bundle within one quarter. TelcoX wins the mid-market and family segments,
but needs to move fast and lock in subscribers before PremiumConnect closes the gap.
The orchestrator may suggest structuring it with 24-month contracts to extend the
window before competitive parity is restored.

## Why These Three Scenarios Work as a Proof of Concept

The same three competitors — ValueNet, PremiumConnect, and RegionalPlus — react
completely differently to each move:

| | ValueNet | PremiumConnect | RegionalPlus | TelcoX Outcome |
|--|--|--|--|--|
| **Price cut** | Immediately out-discounts | Ignores — not their segment | Can't match anyway | **Loses** — margin war |
| **Bundle launch** | Can't replicate content deals | Launches counter-bundle within 1 quarter | Can't afford licensing | **Wins short-term** — window closes |
| **Enterprise 5G** | No enterprise capability | No 5G infrastructure | No CapEx budget | **Wins** — uncontested |

This is exactly what the whitepaper argues: AI simulation surfaces second and
third-order competitive dynamics that human planning misses. A price cut looks like
a simple lever — but the simulation shows ValueNet will out-execute it every time.
A bundle looks risky — but the simulation shows only PremiumConnect can respond,
giving TelcoX a 3–6 month window. An enterprise pivot looks conservative — but the
simulation shows no competitor can follow, making it the highest-return move.

## Verdict Logic Reference

| Your Outcome | Better Alternative? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `loses` | any | **REVISE STRATEGY** |
| `neutral` | yes | **REVISE STRATEGY** |
| `neutral` | no | **PROCEED WITH CAUTION** |
| `wins` | yes | **PROCEED WITH CAUTION** |
| `wins` | no | **PROCEED** |

The orchestrator returns `betterAlternative: null` when the current move is already
the optimal play — it only generates an alternative when a genuinely different
approach would produce a materially better outcome.
